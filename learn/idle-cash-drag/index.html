Idle Cash Drag

Idle cash is cash sitting in operating or reserve accounts that could earn more without compromising liquidity. Idle cash drag is the annual yield the company gives up by leaving it there.

The number is often more material than it first appears because it applies to the whole balance, so small rate differences add up.

Simple formula

  • Take the deployable excess balance.
  • Subtract the current yield from the available yield.
  • Multiply the spread by the excess balance to estimate annual drag.
  • Check the answer against policy so the recommendation stays realistic.

What to check

  • Whether balances exceed the operating buffer.
  • Whether the current yield is below available alternatives.
  • Whether some cash could be separated into a reserve. See how much cash to keep.

Why it matters

  • The drag is a real cost even if the cash feels safe.
  • It can justify a treasury review or a new operating policy.
  • It gives the CFO a simple number to compare against the effort of changing behavior.

What a useful recommendation shows

  • A deployable balance, stated clearly.
  • The current yield compared with realistic alternatives.
  • Why the move fits the company's policy.
  • Who has to approve it.

What to watch for

  • Operating cash should not be confused with reserve cash.
  • Yield assumptions should use a real benchmark, not the bank's vague headline rate.
  • Large balances with tiny spreads still create meaningful annual leakage.
