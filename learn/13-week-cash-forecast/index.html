What Is a 13-Week Cash Forecast?

A 13-week cash forecast is the standard short-term liquidity planning tool used by finance teams to track expected cash inflows and outflows over a rolling 90-day window.

Why 13 weeks?

Thirteen weeks is three calendar months — long enough to surface meaningful liquidity risk, short enough to remain actionable. Lenders and investors often request a 13-week forecast during due diligence, covenant compliance reviews, or restructuring. Finance teams use it as a weekly operating cadence to catch shortfalls before they become crises.

What goes into a 13-week cash forecast?

A well-built 13-week forecast tracks three things at the weekly level:

  • Inflows — customer collections, loan draws, asset sales, tax refunds
  • Outflows — payroll, accounts payable, debt service, rent, taxes
  • Opening and closing cash balance — the net position at the start and end of each week

Most teams also add a minimum cash buffer line — the floor below which cash should not fall — and flag weeks where the projected balance goes below that threshold.

Rolling vs. static forecasts

A static forecast is built once and updated manually. A rolling 13-week forecast advances each week: as week one closes, week fourteen is added to the end. Rolling forecasts stay current without requiring a full rebuild and make it easier to track forecast accuracy over time.

Common mistakes finance teams make

  • Forecasting at the monthly level instead of weekly — this masks intra-month shortfalls
  • Using only the general ledger — bank timing differences cause significant variance
  • Not maintaining multiple scenarios — a single base case gives a false sense of certainty
  • Skipping the variance analysis — without comparing actuals to forecast, accuracy never improves
