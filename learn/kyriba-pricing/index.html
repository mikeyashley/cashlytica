Kyriba Pricing: What’s Public and What to Ask For

Short answer: Kyriba does not show a standard license price on the official treasury product pages reviewed for this guide. It invites prospective customers to request a demo and discuss their needs with sales. A reliable budget therefore starts with a scoped quote, not an unsupported online price estimate.

Reviewed October 21, 2026. Public pages and packaging can change; confirm all current commercial terms directly with Kyriba.

What can be confirmed publicly?

Kyriba markets a treasury platform that includes cash management, liquidity planning, bank account management, payments, and risk-related capabilities. Its product page describes connectivity through banks, APIs, and other protocols and states that it connects to more than 10,000 banks. That is a vendor claim about the platform; ask Kyriba to confirm coverage for your specific banks, countries, and services.

The official pages reviewed direct buyers to a demo or sales contact rather than a public rate card. They do not provide a standard price that can be applied to every company.

What can change a treasury software quote?

Do not assume every item below is priced separately or applies to your proposal. Use the list to make the quote comparable and complete.

  • Selected modules: identify the cash, forecasting, bank-account, payment, risk, and reporting workflows in scope.
  • Connectivity: name each bank, country, account type, file or API method, and required connection frequency.
  • Organization size: provide the number of legal entities, bank accounts, currencies, users, and approval paths.
  • Source systems: list ERP, accounting, payroll, and payment systems and specify which data must move between them.
  • Implementation: ask what discovery, configuration, data mapping, testing, training, and go-live support are included.
  • Ongoing service: clarify support levels, maintenance, upgrades, connectivity changes, and any usage-based charges.
  • Contract terms: compare subscription period, renewal uplift, minimum commitment, payment schedule, and exit or data-export terms.

How to calculate first-year and renewal cost

Compare total cost for the same scope and time period. A simple evaluation model is:

First-year cost = subscription + implementation and integration + bank connectivity + training + internal project time + any usage charges.

Renewal-year cost = recurring subscription + recurring connectivity or usage fees + support + expected internal administration.

Internal time belongs in the comparison too. Estimate hours for treasury, IT, accounting, and bank partners during implementation and in a typical month. Keep one-time work separate from recurring work so a low first-year quote does not obscure the cost of operating the system.

A quote request buyers can send

“Please quote the modules and services needed for [number] entities, [number] bank accounts, [number] currencies, and [number] users. Our required workflows are [cash positioning], [forecasting], [bank account management], [payments], and [risk/reporting]. Our banks and systems are [list]. Please separate recurring subscription, implementation, connectivity, training, support, usage charges, renewal terms, and optional items. Also identify any assumptions, exclusions, and services priced by quote.”

Ask for the proposal to demonstrate one real workflow using representative data. For example: reconcile yesterday’s bank position, trace a forecast variance to its source, and show who can approve a payment change. A feature list does not show whether the configured workflow fits your team.

Is Kyriba right for a mid-size company?

Company size alone does not answer the question. A mid-size company with many banks, currencies, entities, and payment controls may need a broad treasury platform. Another company of similar revenue may mainly need dependable cash visibility, a short-term forecast, or help reviewing bank fees. Match the system to required workflows and operating capacity, then compare a priced scope.
