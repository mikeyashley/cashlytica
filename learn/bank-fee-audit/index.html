Bank Fee Audit: What the Review Can Find

A bank fee audit reviews statement charges, transaction volumes, account terms, and balance credits to find items worth checking. Common findings include duplicate fees, pricing that differs from the agreement, paid services that are no longer used, and avoidable payment costs.

A finding is not automatically recoverable savings. A defensible estimate shows the source statement, applicable rate or benchmark, volume, period, assumptions, and what must change for the opportunity to be realized.

What the audit checks

  • Wire transfer fees above benchmark
  • Account analysis and service charges
  • Duplicate monthly fees after account changes
  • Dormant services that were never shut off
  • Rate drift after a contract renewal

How to validate a possible savings finding

Use a traceable calculation: statement volume × (charged unit price − supported comparison price) × periods. For example, four outbound wires in a month at $35 each versus an illustrative $25 comparison produce a $40 monthly difference, or $480 annualized if the volume and pricing remain constant. That is a possible opportunity, not a refund or guaranteed reduction.

Check the bank agreement and amendments before calling a line an overcharge. If using an external benchmark, match the service type, transaction method, volume, region, and statement period. Separate confirmed billing discrepancies from items that require a negotiation or operational change.
